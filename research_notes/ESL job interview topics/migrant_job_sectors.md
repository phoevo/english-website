# Occupations and Sectors Targeted by Adult ESL Learners and Migrants (UK, US, Canada, Australia, Ireland)

Research date: 14 Nov 2026. Method note: web searches worked, but direct fetches of several primary sites failed because those hosts are outside this session's allowed network (migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk, canada.ca, bls.gov, jobsandskills.gov.au). Many figures below therefore come from search-result summaries of primary documents, or from secondary sources (law firms, trade press). They are labelled. Before publishing, check key numbers against the primary source.

## Q1. Which occupations have the highest share or number of migrant / non-native-English-speaking workers in each country?

### Takeaway
In all five countries, migrants are concentrated at two ends of the labour market. At the lower-wage, entry-level end: care and healthcare support, hospitality and food service, cleaning and admin/support services, warehouse, production and transport. At the professional end: nurses, doctors, IT and engineering. Health and care is the single most consistent sector across all five countries. Hospitality and food service comes next, then logistics, production and cleaning.

### Cited Findings

**UK**
- In Dec 2024, 19% (5.75 million) of UK employees were adult migrants. A later update puts the share of employees who were non-UK nationals at NI registration at 20% in Dec 2025. — [Migration Observatory 2025 briefing](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/2025-Briefing-Migrants-in-the-UK-labour-market-an-overview.pdf); [2026 briefing](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/2026-Briefing-Migrants-in-the-UK-labour-market-an-overview.pdf) (via search summary)
- The 2026 Migration Observatory briefing finds migrants overrepresented in **administrative and support services, hospitality, and health and care**. Recent growth in migrant employment came mainly from recruitment into health and care, especially care work. — [Migration Observatory 2026 briefing](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/2026-Briefing-Migrants-in-the-UK-labour-market-an-overview.pdf)
- For EU-born workers, many of the largest occupations (2021 data, older) were low-wage jobs such as **warehousing, cleaning and care**. Non-EU migrants were comparatively more likely to work in **retail, care and medical professions**. Workers born in the EU8/EU2 countries were the group most likely to be in low-skilled occupations. — [Migration Observatory overview](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/migrants-in-the-uk-labour-market-an-overview)
- Construction is *not* a migrant-heavy UK sector. Madeleine Sumption (Migration Observatory) said about 85% of construction workers were UK-born. — [The National, Feb 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2026/02/18/families-of-migrant-workers-drive-real-change-in-uk-workforce-report-finds)
- Adult social care in England, 2025/26: about 1.59 million people employed. Non-British nationals hold roughly one in three posts, rising to **42% of care workers**. This is a secondary source and is not verified against Skills for Care's own report. — [Access Group blog](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/blog/hsc-international-care-workers-in-the-uk/). An earlier Care England briefing gave 19% "from overseas", which conflicts, probably because it uses a different definition or year. — [Care England](https://www.careengland.org.uk/social-cares-improving-workforce-figures-mask-a-growing-concern-with-no-plan-to-address/)

**US**
- BLS, 2025 data (released 19 May 2026): foreign-born workers were more likely than native-born workers to work in **service occupations** (21.2%; native-born about 15%, approximate from the snippet). They were also more likely to work in **natural resources, construction and maintenance**, and in **production, transportation and material moving**. They were less likely to work in management/professional or sales/office jobs. — [BLS Foreign-born workers 2025](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/forbrn_05192026.htm)
- 2024 BLS data (prior year): 13.9% of foreign-born workers were in natural resources, construction and maintenance, against 7.7% of native-born. In production, transportation and material moving, the figures were 15.5% against 11.6%. — [BLS 2024 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/forbrn_05202025.htm)
- MPI (2023 data): immigrant-origin workers (immigrants plus their US-born children) were 47.6 million, or 29% of US workers. They were 36% of **food and personal services** workers, 34% of **health-care support** and **blue-collar** workers, and 38% of **STEM** workers. These four groups employed 55% of immigrant-origin workers. — [MPI, Future of Work report](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/research/future-work-immigrant-origin-workers-us-economy)

**Canada**
- Immigrants make up 42% of **nurse aides and related occupations** (2021 Census, per IRCC). — [IRCC, immigration and the economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/immigration-economy.html). This conflicts with 35% given by [CIC News (2023)](https://cicnews.com/2023/11/how-immigration-has-benefitted-canadian-sectors-1139585.html).
- Immigrants are 35% of workers in **truck transportation** and 56% of truck transportation business owners. — [IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/immigration-economy.html)
- Among immigrants who landed in 2018–19, 35% were in lower-skilled or labourer jobs in May 2021, almost as many as the 40% in higher-skilled jobs. The lower-skilled jobs named include **nurse aides, transport truck drivers, food and beverage servers, and retail salespersons**. Recent immigrants are more likely to be nurse aides, orderlies and patient service associates than registered nurses. — [Statistics Canada, Economic and Social Reports 2024](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2024009/article/00005-eng.htm)

**Australia**
- In 2025, **accommodation and food services** had the highest share of recent migrants: 14.3% of the workforce, up from 9.7% in 2015. Recent migrants held 14.5% of **hospitality worker** roles. Migrants often make up over 10% of the workforce in key **care and industrial** roles on the national shortage list. Recent migrants hold 21.3% of professional roles, especially **engineering, IT, healthcare and accounting**. This is a secondary analysis, not ABS. — [Ai Group, 2026](https://www.australianindustrygroup.com.au/resourcecentre/research-economics/economics-intelligence/2026/the-contribution-of-recent-migrants-to-australias-workforce/)
- ABS, 2021–22 (older): **Health care and social assistance** was the most common industry for migrants (15.0% of migrant-held jobs). — [ABS Jobs in Australia 2021–22](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/jobs/jobs-australia/2021-22)
- ABS Characteristics of Recent Migrants, 2019 (older): the most common occupation for Skilled-stream migrants was Business, HR and Marketing Professionals (10%). **Carers and Aides** employed 10% of Family-stream and 14% of Humanitarian-stream migrants. — [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/two-thirds-recent-migrants-employed)

**Ireland**
- In 2024, non-Irish nationals were 27.5% of all employees (26.2% in 2023). They made up **45.6% of administrative and support services** employees and **45.1% of accommodation and food services** employees, the two most migrant-heavy sectors. Accommodation and food also had the lowest median weekly earnings (€391.62). — [CSO, Distribution of Earnings by Nationality 2024](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-den/distributionofearningsbynationality2024); [CSO Business in Ireland 2025](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-biilmss/businessinireland2025-labourmarketandsocialsustainability/laboursupply/)
- 32.2% of Indian nationals worked in **human health and social work** in 2024. 30.2% of Ukrainian nationals' employments in 2023 were in **accommodation and food**. — [CSO](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-den/distributionofearningsbynationality2024)

### Inferences
- The same sectors recur in all five countries. A ranking for lesson priority, by size of migrant workforce:
  1. Health and social care: care worker, healthcare assistant, nurse aide, nurse.
  2. Hospitality and food service: server, kitchen staff, hotel housekeeping, barista.
  3. Cleaning and admin/support services.
  4. Warehouse, logistics, delivery and truck driving.
  5. Production and food processing.
  6. Retail.
  7. Professional and skilled-visa roles: IT/software, engineering, accounting, doctors.
  8. Construction and trades, which matter strongly in the US and Canada, much less in the UK.
- Administrative and support services covers cleaning, security and agency/temp work. It is migrant-heavy in both the UK and Ireland, which suggests cleaner, security guard and agency-worker interviews are worth covering.

### Gaps
- I could not get occupation-level (4-digit SOC/NOC/ANZSCO) rankings of the jobs with the most migrant workers for any country. Primary tables could not be fetched: ONS APS, BLS table 4, StatCan Census by NOC, JSA "Australian Labour Market for Migrants April 2026".
- No reliable data was found on non-native English speakers specifically, as opposed to foreign-born workers. Foreign-born figures include native speakers from the US, UK, Australia and elsewhere.

## Q2. Which roles are on skilled-worker / shortage lists or have big international recruitment pipelines?

### Takeaway
Healthcare is the biggest recruitment pipeline in every country: nurses, doctors, care/support workers and allied health. Trades and construction are a pipeline in Canada, Australia and Ireland. IT and engineering are a pipeline in Ireland and Australia. In 2025–26 the UK and Canada sharply tightened their lower-skilled routes, while Ireland expanded its lists.

### Cited Findings
**UK**
- From 22 July 2025 the minimum skill level for Skilled Worker sponsorship went back to RQF 6 (graduate level), dropping about 180 occupations. A Temporary Shortage List of about 50 mid-skill (RQF 3–5) occupations was created, valid only for Certificates of Sponsorship issued before 31 Dec 2026, and with no dependants for RQF 3–5 roles. — [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/7/uks-1-july-2025-statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules); [Macfarlanes](https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102lq1m/navigating-the-new-skill-levels-for-the-uk-skilled-worker-visa/)
- Year ending June 2026: work visa grants fell 30% year on year and 76% compared with the year ending Dec 2023. The fall was driven by the Health and Care Worker and Skilled Worker routes. The largest drops were in caring personal service and nursing professional occupations. — [Home Office, Why do people come to the UK? Work (YE June 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-work); [YE March 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-march-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-work)
- In the year to June 2026, generalist medical practitioners (SOC 2211) were the top occupation for overseas-applicant grants, at 3,126. This comes from a third-party tracker of Home Office table Occ_D02. — [Hunt UK Visa Sponsors](https://huntukvisasponsors.com/skilled-worker-visa-statistics)
- Dental nurses were effectively removed from the route: 6 grants in Q4 2025–Q2 2026, against 122 in the previous three quarters. — [Dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/?p=461349)

**Canada**
- IRCC set its 2026 Express Entry category-based priorities on 18 Feb 2026. **Healthcare and social services** and **trades** remain. New 2026 categories reported include physicians with Canadian experience, researchers, senior managers, transport and military. — [CIC News](https://www.cicnews.com/2025/12/these-are-the-biggest-changes-coming-to-express-entry-in-2026-1263223.html); [Redim](https://redim.ca/5-new-express-entry-category-based-draws-will-run-in-2026-targeting-doctors-researchers-managers-transport-and-military/)
- There were 3 healthcare draws in 2026 up to 16 Sept, with CRS cut-offs of 467–475. About 14,500 invitations have gone out through healthcare draws, the most after French-language draws. The first physicians draw (19 Feb 2026) invited 391 doctors with a CRS cut-off of 169. **Cooks (NOC 63200)** were removed from the trades category for 2026. These are secondary sources. — [Moving2Canada](https://moving2canada.com/news/2026-express-entry-categories/); [CIC News Feb 2026](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/02/three-new-express-entry-categories-0271729.html/amp)
- In 2025, category-based draws were limited to French, Education, Healthcare and Trades. — [CIC News](https://www.cicnews.com/2025/12/these-are-the-types-of-candidates-ircc-is-focusing-on-in-2026-1263531.html)

**Australia**
- The Core Skills Occupation List (456 occupations) underpins the Skills in Demand (subclass 482) Core Skills stream and the 186 Direct Entry stream. It replaced the MLTSSL, STSOL and ROL lists in Dec 2024. JSA ran a 2025 review consultation. Reported Core Skills Income Threshold from 1 July 2026: AUD 79,423 or 79,499, depending on the source. — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/australia-draft-core-skills-occupation-list-for-proposed-skills-in-demand-visa-released.html); [ThisIsAustralia](https://www.thisisaustralia.com/news/skilled-migration-2026-csol-update/); [JSA 2025 Skills in Demand report](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/download/19955/2025-skills-demand-sid-report/4016/2025-skills-demand-sid-report/pdf)
- The National Occupation Shortage List found shortages in 29% of assessed occupations. This is a secondary source. — [ThisIsAustralia](https://www.thisisaustralia.com/news/skilled-migration-2026-csol-update/)

**Ireland**
- In 2025, about 31,044 employment permits were issued, down 20% on 2024. **Health and social care** had 26% (7,948), **information and communication** 12%, and **accommodation and food** 11%. Agriculture had 1,037 permits in H1 2025. — [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/arid-41932158.html); [IrishTalents tracker](https://irishtalents.com/ireland-employment-permits/2025); [Agriland](https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/1037-work-permits-issued-to-agri-sector-in-first-half-of-year); official source: [DETE permit statistics 2025](https://www.enterprise.gov.ie/en/publications/employment-permit-statistics-2025.html)
- In May 2026, following the 2025 review, 32 amendments were made to the eligible occupation lists, targeting **construction, healthcare, transport and agri-food**. Critical Skills additions include roles such as construction planner/scheduler, community eye care, geospatial surveyor, riggers, agronomist and IP professionals. Sources differ on the count, either 4 or 6. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en-IE/insights/2026/05/27/irelands-employment-permit-occupations-lists-whats-changed-in-may-2026); [EIG Law](https://eiglaw.com/ireland-expands-employment-permit-eligibility-in-construction-healthcare-and-transport/); [DETE review report](https://www.enterprise.gov.ie/en/publications/publication-files/report-of-the-review-of-the-occupations-lists-for-employment-permits-2025.pdf)
- Critical Skills Employment Permit salary minimums: €38,000 (with a relevant degree), or €64,000 for experience-based applicants. — [DETE consultation page](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/consultations/public-consultation-review-of-occupations-lists-for-employment-permits.html)

**US**
- No single shortage list was researched. See Q4 for the policy context.

### Inferences
- For skilled-visa learners, priority interview sets are: **nurse** (UK, Ireland, Australia, Canada), **doctor/physician**, **software/IT** (Ireland, Australia, UK), **engineer**, **skilled trades** (electrician, plumber, welder, carpenter; Canada and Australia), **healthcare support / personal support worker** (Canada), and **truck driver** (Canada transport category, Ireland transport).

### Gaps
- No verified US H-1B or H-2A/H-2B occupation breakdown was gathered.
- The exact 2026 CSOL changes and the current status of the UK Temporary Shortage List were not confirmed from primary sources.

## Q3. Which entry-level jobs do refugees and newcomers typically get first?

### Takeaway
Refugees and newcomers with limited English usually start in entry-level service, factory, warehouse, hospitality, food-processing and care-aide jobs, usually within 3 to 6 months of arrival.

### Cited Findings
- US resettlement agencies say newcomers "begin their employment… with entry level positions, often in service related or factory jobs". Typical sectors listed: **warehouse and fulfillment, hospitality, manufacturing, food services**. — [Lutheran Social Service of Minnesota](https://www.lssmn.org/services/refugees/services/employment)
- An FPI/Tent study (2020, older) of refugee employers in manufacturing, **hotels, health care, meatpacking, transportation and construction** found refugee turnover in manufacturing of 4%, against 11% for the industry. 73% of firms reported higher retention for refugees. — [WEF summary](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2020/05/hiring-refugees-report-economic-sense/); [Assembly Magazine](https://www.assemblymag.com/articles/94354-study-refugees-stay-in-manufacturing-jobs-longer-than-other-employees?v=preview)
- First jobs are often "repetitive and menial" because of limited English, according to a Kansas City nonprofit. — [Yahoo/KC news](https://news.yahoo.com/many-kansas-city-refugees-job-100000978.html)
- Employment targets: a World Relief office aims for employment within 3–6 months. US Matching Grant targets self-sufficiency in 120–180 days. — [World Relief](https://worldrelief.org/content/uploads/2025/03/QC_Employment-Services-Enrollment-FY25.pdf)
- In Australia, 14% of Humanitarian-stream migrants worked as **Carers and Aides** (ABS 2019, older). — [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/two-thirds-recent-migrants-employed)
- In Canada, recent immigrants are concentrated in nurse aides, truck drivers, food and beverage servers and retail salespersons. — [StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2024009/article/00005-eng.htm)
- In Ireland, Ukrainian arrivals are concentrated in accommodation and food (30.2% in 2023). — [CSO](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-den/distributionofearningsbynationality2024)

### Inferences
- Beginner and lower-intermediate interview lessons should prioritise warehouse/picker-packer, factory and food-processing operative, cleaner/housekeeper, kitchen porter and food service, retail/cashier, care aide, and delivery driver.

### Gaps
- No UK or Canadian resettlement-programme employment outcome reports by occupation were found.

## Q4. Notable recent changes (2024–2026) that shift demand

### Takeaway
The UK closed overseas care-worker recruitment in July 2025 and raised the skill floor. Canada restricted low-wage TFW hiring and refocused Express Entry. The US cut refugee admissions to a record low and ended TPS for many people. Ireland expanded its permit lists. The result is that more learners are applying for jobs **from inside the country**, with domestic employers, rather than through overseas recruitment.

### Cited Findings
- **UK:** Overseas recruitment for care workers (SOC 6135) and senior care workers (SOC 6136) closed on 22 July 2025 (HC 997). In-country switching is allowed until 22 July 2028. No reversal had been announced as of 2026. — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uk-care-worker-visa-route-closes); [Irwin Mitchell](https://www.irwinmitchell.com/news-and-insights/expert-comment/post/102kr4p/care-worker-visa-route-to-close-major-immigration-reform-effective-22-July-2025); [DavidsonMorris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/overseas-care-senior-care-worker-visa-closes-22-july-2025/)
- **UK care:** International arrivals into direct care fell from 105,000 (2023/24) to 50,000 (2024/25) to 30,000 (2025/26). About 95% of international recruits are now people already in the UK on dependant, family or student visas. The vacancy rate is 6.2%, the lowest since 2015/16. — [Care Workers' Charity on Skills for Care 2025/26](https://www.thecareworkerscharity.org.uk/?p=20025); [Care England](https://www.careengland.org.uk/social-cares-improving-workforce-figures-mask-a-growing-concern-with-no-plan-to-address/)
- **UK:** The RQF 6 threshold from 22 July 2025 (about 180 occupations dropped). Skilled Worker applications are down sharply in 2026. — [Workplace Journal, Jul 2026](https://workplacejournal.co.uk/2026/07/skilled-worker-visa-applications-fall-by-39-in-a-year-home-office/)
- **Canada:** Low-wage LMIA refusal-to-process applies in many census metropolitan areas. On 10 April 2026, Vancouver, Winnipeg and Halifax were added to 27 others, through 9 July 2026. Quebec froze Montreal and Laval. A 10% cap applies to low-wage TFWs (rural employers allowed up to 15% from March 2026). TFWP arrivals hit a two-year low in late 2025 and early 2026. — [HCAmag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/specialization/immigration/ottawa-freezes-low-wage-lmia-applications-for-3-new-cities/571550); [CIC News Jan 2026](https://www.cicnews.com/2026/01/arrivals-under-temporary-foreign-worker-program-drop-to-lowest-level-in-two-years-0169213.html); [CIC News Quebec](https://www.cicnews.com/2025/11/quebec-extends-freeze-on-low-wage-tfwp-work-permit-processing-1161871.html)
- **US:** The FY2026 refugee ceiling was set at 7,500, a record low, and later raised to 17,500 by emergency determination in May 2026. Admissions were 7,730 as of 30 June 2026 (CRS). Pew gives just over 10,000, against about 38,000 in FY2025. — [CRS IF13277](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13277.html); [Global Refuge](https://www.globalrefuge.org/news/refugee-cap-finalized-at-record-low-7500-for-fy-2026/); [Stateline, Aug 2026](https://stateline.org/2026/08/17/refugee-admissions-on-track-to-hit-record-low/)
- **US:** Nearly 1 million noncitizens lost TPS and work authorisation. Elder-care employers, for example, report dismissals. — [American Immigration Council](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/fact-sheet/employment-authorization-changes/)
- **Ireland:** The May 2026 list expansion covers construction, healthcare, transport and agri-food (see Q2). Permits issued in 2025 were down 20%. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en-IE/insights/2026/05/27/irelands-employment-permit-occupations-lists-whats-changed-in-may-2026)

### Inferences
- UK care-job demand from ESL learners continues, but the applicants are now mainly people already in the UK: students, dependants and switchers. Lessons should cover in-country applications, such as switching sponsor or a first UK care job, rather than overseas recruitment interviews.
- New US refugee arrivals are much lower, so US entry-level ESL interview demand will come more from existing immigrants than from new refugees.
- Canada's newcomer demand is moving towards healthcare, trades and permanent-resident-track roles, and away from low-wage TFW roles in cities.

### Gaps
- Canada's international student permit caps (2024–26) and their effect on student jobs were not researched in the time available. The same applies to Australia's student-visa caps and the UK Graduate visa shortening.
- No 2026 US occupation data on the effects of the immigration crackdown was found.
