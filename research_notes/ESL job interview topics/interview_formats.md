# Job interview formats and question types for non-native speakers (B1–C1 ESL lesson input)

Research note: done 14 Nov 2026. Several hosts would not resolve in this session (languageonthemove.com, newdiversities.mmg.mpg.de, kclpure.kcl.ac.uk, f1jobs.io), so I could not read the full texts of Roberts' papers. Findings below come from search-result summaries of the listed pages. Where a summary may be off, I say so.

## Q1. Which formats and question types do non-native speakers struggle with, and why? (Includes research on linguistic and cultural bias)

### Takeaway
The strongest research, from Celia Roberts and colleagues in the UK, finds that competency-based interviews ("Tell me about a time when...") put a "linguistic penalty" on migrants. Candidates are judged on whether they can produce a hybrid of personal, institutional and professional talk, and on how they present foreign work experience. The judgement is not really about their English or their skills. Career-service advice agrees on the main cultural problems: self-promotion and "I" statements, open questions like "Tell me about yourself", eye contact, and small talk.

### Cited Findings
**Gatekeeping and linguistic penalty research**
- Roberts' 2012 *Diversities* article, "Translating global experience into institutional models of competency: linguistic inequalities in the job interview", draws on 61 video-recorded UK interviews for low-paid jobs. It argues that the selection interview creates a linguistic penalty for some migrant groups, especially in how foreign work experience (FWE) is recognised or dismissed. It also argues that interview language helps produce inequality and hides the gap between apparent fairness and unequal outcomes. — [Diversities 2012 (PDF)](https://newdiversities.mmg.mpg.de/fileadmin/user_upload/2012_14-02_art4.pdf); [abstract page](https://newdiversities.mmg.mpg.de/?p=1997)
- Roberts says the penalty is "rendered invisible by the processes and rhetoric of institutional selection". She reports that applicants from overseas, including migrants and international medical graduates, do much worse than both white British and British BME candidates. — [Roberts, AEDEAN plenary "Institutions and linguistic penalties" (PDF)](https://aedean.org/pdf_atatimecrisis/PLENARIA_Roberts_AEDEAN35.pdf); [KCL copy](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/files/5956317/PLENARIA_Roberts_AEDEAN35.pdf)
- Roberts' book *Linguistic Penalties and the Job Interview* (Equinox, 2021) has chapters on how current interviews are built around competencies and "soft skills". It also covers migrant candidates, who face extra demands on their "interpretive and cohesive resources". — [Equinox chapter page](https://www.equinoxpub.com/home/view-chapter/?id=25081); [Migrant Candidates and the Linguistic Penalty](https://equinoxpub.com/home/view-chapter/?id=25082); [Researching Ethnic and Linguistic Penalties](https://www.equinoxpub.com/home/view-chapter/?id=25076)
- Campbell & Roberts (2007), "Migration, ethnicity and competing discourses in the job interview: synthesizing the institutional and personal", *Discourse & Society* 18(3): 243–271. Recruiters felt that ethnic-minority and overseas candidates were unsuccessful at combining personal discourse (who I am, my motivation) with institutional discourse (the organisation's competency vocabulary). The same research appeared earlier as *Talk on Trial: Job Interviews, Language and Ethnicity* (2006). — [Benjamins citation context](https://benjamins.com/online/ebop/publications/46390); [Roberts AEDEAN plenary](https://aedean.org/pdf_atatimecrisis/PLENARIA_Roberts_AEDEAN35.pdf)
- A 2014 Language on the Move summary of Roberts' chapter "The Gatekeeping of Babel" says the linguistic and cultural nature of the assessment stays unacknowledged and invisible. — [Language on the Move](https://languageonthemove.com/linguistic-penalty-in-the-job-interview/)
- Background: *Crosstalk* (1979) was a BBC film with notes by Gumperz, Jupp & Roberts, published by the National Centre for Industrial Language Training in Southall. It showed cross-cultural misunderstanding in transactions, including "job applicant / appointing committee", caused by different intonation patterns and cultural norms. The best-known intonation example (the Heathrow canteen "gravy" case) is not an interview. Another finding is that when candidates did not give extended answers, interviewers became more controlling of their turns. — [LGPolicy list summary](https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/lgpolicy-list/2005-September/002329.html); [WGBH "gravy"](https://www.wgbh.org/news/2014-04-16/sometimes-getting-along-comes-down-to-how-you-say-gravy); [Benjamins Handbook: Workplace interaction](https://lwc1.benjamins.com/online/hop/articles/wor4)

**"Tell me about yourself" and open questions**
- Canadian HR reporting says traditional open prompts can screen out talented immigrants, because Canadian-born candidates already know the format and what a strong answer contains. — [HR Reporter](https://hrreporter.com/news/hr-news/evaluating-new-canadians-requires-new-tactics/311915)
- World Education Services (WES) guidance for employers warns that "Tell me about yourself" can produce a response that doesn't give the information the interviewer wants. It recommends asking about specific education and experience instead. — [WES, Interviewing Immigrant Candidates (PDF)](https://www.wes.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/0622-4-interviewing-immigrant-candidates-v4.pdf)
- Canadian guidance notes that immigrants may not know expectations such as keeping eye contact and talking with pride about past professional achievements. It advises candidates to talk about their international experience rather than apologise for lacking Canadian experience. — [immigration.ca](https://immigration.ca/interview-techniques-can-eliminate-hidden-cultural-biases/); [University of Manitoba resource](https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/bitstreams/fd440917-f304-4e03-a84a-12afa31793ef/download)

**Behavioural/STAR questions and self-promotion**
- Kellogg (Northwestern) career staff say international students often feel self-promotion is "bragging" and goes against their cultural norms. — [Kellogg blog](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/news/blog/2021/04/16/international-student-recruiting/)
- STAR is built around individual achievement. Students from cultures that value humility and group success find "I" statements hard, and deflecting credit to the team reads as low confidence, not modesty. — [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/international-students-interview-prep-career-centers/); [uni2study](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/us-job-interview-norms-and-behavioral-questions-for-international-students)
- University career centres such as JMU and UConn teach STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result), advise eye contact, and use "sell it vs tell it" practice. — [JMU international students](https://www.jmu.edu/career/students/communities/international.shtml); [JMU cultural barriers](https://www.jmu.edu/career/students/search/cultural-barriers.shtml); [UConn](https://career.uconn.edu/resources/interview-tips-for-international-students/); [NCDA](https://ncda.org/aws/NCDA/pt/sd/news_article/92291/_self/CC_layout_details/true)
- Advisers suggest practising "bragging" first with peers who understand the cultural awkwardness. They also suggest reframing self-promotion as specific facts stated calmly, with credit to others where it is due. — [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/international-students-interview-prep-career-centers/); [f1jobs, US interview culture](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/us-job-interview-culture-international-candidates)

**Values-based questions** (see also Q3)
- Values-based recruitment (VBR) tries to identify the values, beliefs and attitudes that are fundamental to a professional. One approach is to take each organisational value and write two or three questions for it. — [RCNi](https://stg-v2.rcni.com/nursing-management/evidence-and-practice/clinical/improving-quality-nhs-workforce-through-values); [MMU VBR guide (PDF)](https://www.mmu.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2022-06/Values-Based%20Recruitment.pdf)
- Behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time...") are said to work better than scenario questions in VBR. — [Flourish](https://flourish.co.uk/community/care-support-worker-interview-questions/)

**Accent bias**
- York University research reports that workers with non-native accents are often discriminated against at work, and it separates accent from English competence or fluency. — [YorkU news](https://yorku.ca/news/?p=18915); [HR Law Canada](https://hrlawcanada.com/2023/12/how-benevolent-sexism-undermines-asian-women-with-foreign-accents-in-the-workplace/)

### Inferences
- The difficulty is less about grammar than about genre. Competency answers need a specific personal narrative in past tenses, mapped onto the employer's competency or values vocabulary ("teamwork", "compassion", "respect and dignity"). B1–C1 learners should practise this mapping explicitly: name the value, then give a "time when" story, then the result, then what they learned.
- Foreign work experience needs reframing language, such as "In my role at X, which is similar to a [UK equivalent]...". Roberts' work suggests that otherwise it is discounted.
- Short answers can make interviewers take control and close down turns (the Crosstalk/Gumperz finding). Teaching learners how long an answer should be and how to extend it (roughly 1–2 minutes, with examples) is worthwhile.
- Several lesson topics are shown only through advice-site consensus, not research: salary questions, "Do you have any questions for us?" and small talk.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full texts of Roberts' work, so I have no exact pass-rate statistics by group. Check the 2012 *Diversities* PDF directly; summaries suggest it gives outcome differences across the 61 interviews.
- I found no research-quality source on salary-negotiation difficulty for non-native speakers, or on "questions to ask the interviewer". Only general advice exists.
- I found no UK study testing "Tell me about yourself" against non-native or immigrant candidates. The Canadian sources are practitioner guidance.

## Q2. Phone, video and one-way recorded (HireVue-style) interviews: what extra challenges do non-native speakers face?

### Takeaway
Audio-only and video formats remove visual cues and add lag and audio-quality problems, which hurt comprehension and turn-taking. One-way (asynchronous) interviews remove any chance to ask for clarification and add time pressure. Evidence of AI accent bias is thin: it is mainly vendor claims, one commissioned audit and anecdotes.

### Cited Findings
- Oxford careers guidance on telephone and video interviews: look into the camera, allow for lag between video and audio, don't interrupt before the question is finished, and keep a backup plan (for example a phone number) in case the connection fails. — [Oxford Careers Service](https://www.careers.ox.ac.uk/node/768726)
- Coaching sources say audio-only formats remove facial cues and struggle with accents and audio quality. They advise slowing your normal pace by about 10–15% for clearer pronunciation. They also suggest replacing apologies for lost words with a redirection. — [TalkDrill](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/interview/remote-job-interview-english-tips/); [Talaera](https://www.talaera.com/speaking/job-interview-tips/)
- A January 2026 *ESP Today* article (Fei-Wen Cheng) on EFL candidates in asynchronous video interviews reports that the challenges of this format can be particularly acute for EFL learners. — [ESP Today, Jan 2026 (PDF)](https://www.esptodayjournal.org/pdf/january_2026/1_Fei-Wen_Cheng.pdf)
- HireVue says it tested transcription accuracy for native and non-native speakers and found its speech-to-text vendor "robust". It also says it removed non-language inputs (for example visual analysis) from its models. These are vendor claims. — [HireVue blog](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/hiring/the-latest-leap-in-hirevues-assessment-technology)
- A 2020/21 ORCAA algorithmic audit, commissioned by HireVue, named group score gaps as a key fairness risk. HireVue said it planned research on accents and video-assessed competency scores. — [ORCAA audit (PDF)](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/oneil-risk-consulting-and-algorithmic-auditing-01-2021.pdf)
- A 2025 Tilburg study in the *European Journal of Work and Organizational Psychology* found that standardised evaluation in asynchronous video interviews reduced sex-related bias but not bias linked to other characteristics of the interviewee. — [Tilburg research portal](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/assessing-biasing-factors-in-asynchronous-video-interviews-applic/)
- Anecdote: a Harvard D3 post describes a non-native speaker getting a low "speech clarity" score from an AI speech-analysis tool. — [Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-peopleanalytics/submission/can-an-ai-recruiting-platform-be-biased-towards-disabled-people/index.html)
- A UXPA opinion piece argues that non-native speakers may score lower in AI interviews because they phrase things differently from native speakers. — [UXPA magazine](https://oldmagazine.uxpa.org/?p=13739)
- A candidate guide notes that HireVue's own materials do not say whether accent is scored. — [f1jobs](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/hirevue-one-way-video-interview-non-native-speakers)

### Inferences
- Lessons should cover:
  - phone and video repair language: "Sorry, you cut out — could you repeat the last part?" and "I think there's a slight delay on the line."
  - managing silence and turn-taking under lag
  - one-way recording skills: using the prep time to outline STAR answers, keeping to a time limit (often 1–3 minutes), and speaking to a camera with no feedback. Learners cannot ask for clarification, so they need to paraphrase the question back into the answer ("You've asked about a time I...").

### Gaps
- I found no independent peer-reviewed study of accent bias in HireVue-style scoring. The planned HireVue accent study did not come up in my searches.
- I could not confirm the typical prep time, answer length or retake rules on official HireVue candidate pages. These vary by employer.

## Q3. Sector-specific formats in roles migrants commonly enter

### Takeaway
Many sectors where migrants work use set formats. Health and care use values-based panels and scenario questions. Nurses take the NMC OSCE and doctors the PLAB route, and OET role-plays assess clinical communication. Retail uses assessment days with group tasks, hospitality uses trial shifts, schools ask safer-recruitment safeguarding questions, IT uses "think aloud" live coding, and trades focus on safety and cards (CSCS). Visa and right-to-work questions run through all of them, and the UK closed overseas recruitment of care workers in July 2025.

### Cited Findings
**NHS nursing: values-based interviews and NMC OSCE**
- NHS constitutional values and the 6Cs of nursing are assessed throughout the OSCE at every station. Applicants can sit the OSCE up to three times in one application, and the CBT must be passed as part of the NMC process. — [NHS Employers OSCE information](https://www.nhsemployers.org/your-workforce/recruit/employer-led-recruitment/international-recruitment/immigration-rules-and-the-points-based-system/overseas-nurses-and-midwives/osce-information); [NHS Employers overseas nurses](https://www.nhsemployers.org/articles/recruitment-overseas-nurses-and-midwives)
- HEE's managers' guide says the CBT covers four NMC domains, including professional values. — [HEE Managers Guide (PDF)](https://www.hee.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/documents/Managers%20Guide%20International%20Recruitment.pdf)
- An NHS Professionals international support pack says most trusts use competency-based interviews that ask how you have used particular skills in past roles. — [NHSP application support pack (PDF)](https://www.nhsprofessionals.nhs.uk/-/media/corporate/nhsp-international/nursing-and-midwifery-application-support-pack.pdf)
- Candidates report panel interviews of about three people and values-based hiring for London staff nurse posts, with questions like "Why do you want to work for the NHS?" and "How do you show compassion?". — [Glassdoor NHS review](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/NHS-Interview-E12873-RVW92900589.htm); [MockQuestions Band 5](https://www.mockquestions.com/position/NHS+Band+5+Nurse/Do-you-speak-any-foreign-languages-fluently-3394552.html)
- Mersey Care NHS publishes values-based questions, for example "Tell me about a time you made a difference to someone's day" and "Describe a time you needed to offer emotional support to another person". — [Mersey Care values-based questions](https://merseycare.nhs.uk/values-based-questions)
- HEE guidance notes that overseas nurses are not always comfortable speaking up in front of others, and suggests building their confidence. — [HEE Managers Guide (PDF)](https://www.hee.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/documents/Managers%20Guide%20International%20Recruitment.pdf)

**OET Speaking role-plays**
- OET Speaking has an unassessed warm-up (about 3–4 minutes), then two role-plays. Each role-play has 3 minutes of preparation and about 5 minutes of speaking. The candidate plays the health professional and the interlocutor plays the patient or carer. — [OET official, Speaking](https://oet.com/learn/speaking); [Specialist Language Courses](https://specialistlanguagecourses.com/oet-speaking-test-tips/)
- There are nine criteria:
  - 4 linguistic: intelligibility, fluency, appropriateness of language, and resources of grammar and expression
  - 5 clinical communication: relationship building, understanding and incorporating the patient's perspective, providing structure, information gathering, and information giving
  Grade B typically means about 5/6 on the linguistic criteria and 2/3 on the clinical communication criteria. Two assessors rate a recording. — [OET criteria overview](https://oet.com/post/speaking-criteria-overview); [EduBenchmark](https://edubenchmark.com/blog/oet-speaking-role-play-cards/)

**Doctors (IMGs)**
- IMGs show differential attainment in postgraduate exams and progression. GMC-commissioned work found contributing factors including unfamiliarity with UK systems and cultural norms, and cultural differences affecting relationships at work. — [Woolf et al. 2016, BMJ Open (UCL)](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1527438/1/e013429.full.pdf); [GMC Fair Training Pathways (PDF)](https://www.scotlanddeanery.nhs.scot/media/547232/gmc-fair-training-pathways-for-all-understanding-experiences-of-progression-1.pdf); [BJPsych Bulletin 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8477154)
- One study suggests that excessive weight may be given to interview performance in specialty recruitment. — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549602)
- NES training material lists communication problems, culture shock and low psychological safety among IMG challenges. — [NES slides (PDF)](https://www.appraisal.nes.scot.nhs.uk/media/1p4hn4zm/04_slides.pdf); [HEE East of England, helping IMGs (PDF)](https://heeoe.hee.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/vijay_-_helping_imgs_achieve_their_potential.pdf)

**Adult social care: values-based recruitment**
- In 2013 the National Skills Academy for Social Care, with Skills for Care and MacIntyre, produced a VBR toolkit. It included guidance, draft job adverts, interview questions and an online personality profiling tool. — [DHSC social care blog](https://socialcare.blog.gov.uk/2013/08/08/recruiting-for-values-in-social-care-a-new-toolkit-for-employers); [Homecare.co.uk](https://www.homecare.co.uk/news/article.cfm/id/1560557/national-skills-academy-for-social-care-pilots-new-values-based-recruitment-kit-for-employers)
- Common care-interview topics are safeguarding adults, motivation ("Why care?"), active listening, empathy, resilience, de-escalation, record-keeping and manual handling. — [Birdie](https://birdie.care/blog/carer-interview-questions-what-should-you-ask); [Randstad care worker questions](https://www.randstad.co.uk/career-advice/career-guidance/top-care-worker-interview-questions-answers-how-to-prepare-for-a-job/); [The Care Workers' Charity](https://www.thecareworkerscharity.org.uk/?p=2856); [CareLearning](https://carelearning.org.uk/care-careers/care-worker-interview-questions-and-answers-with-examples/)
- **Policy context:** On 22 July 2025 the UK closed entry-clearance (overseas) recruitment for care workers (SOC 6135) and senior care workers (SOC 6136) under Skilled Worker, through statement of changes HC 997. In-country switching continues during a transition period to 22 July 2028. The general Skilled Worker threshold rose from £38,700 to £41,700. — [Irwin Mitchell](https://www.irwinmitchell.com/news-and-insights/expert-comment/post/102kr4p/care-worker-visa-route-to-close-major-immigration-reform-effective-22-july-2025); [EIN](https://www.ein.org.uk/news/major-changes-immigration-rules-raise-skilled-worker-thresholds-and-end-overseas-recruitment); [NHS Employers](https://www.nhsemployers.org/news/immigration-rule-changes-announced); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uk-care-worker-visa-route-closes)

**Retail: assessment days and group tasks**
- Supermarket assessment days combine group tasks with one-to-one interviews. Waitrose candidates describe:
  - prioritising a day's problems (missing stock, breakages, unhappy customers) and organising the team
  - matching products to customer needs
  - a situational judgement test that ranks decisions
  — [Glassdoor Waitrose](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Waitrose-Interview-E22047-RVW17051001.htm); [Glassdoor Waitrose 2](https://www.glassdoor.sg/Interview/Waitrose-Interview-E22047-RVW12155723.htm); [Glassdoor Waitrose 3](https://www.glassdoor.ca/Interview/Waitrose-Interview-E22047-RVW16396522.htm)
- Tesco assessors look for speaking clearly, listening to others and stepping up to lead. — [Practice Aptitude Tests](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/what-to-expect-at-a-tesco-assessment-centre/); [TargetJobs retail](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/retail/job-done-how-graduates-can-stand-out-retail-assessment-centres); [Michael Page AU](https://www.michaelpage.com.au/retail-jobs-career-advice/what-happens-retail-assessment-centres)

**Hospitality: trial shifts**
- The UK law on trial shifts is a grey area under the National Minimum Wage Act 1998 and Regulations 2015. A short unpaid trial that is a genuine part of recruitment (a few hours) may not make the person a "worker" entitled to pay. Longer or productive work generally must be paid, and law firms disagree on where the line falls. — [Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2024/dont-fall-foul-of-minimum-wage-with-unpaid-trial-shifts-in-the-hospitality-industry/); [Birketts](https://birketts.co.uk/legal-update/hospitality-focus-should-employers-pay-for-trial-shifts); [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/unpaid-work-trial-shifts-in-the-uk-what-employers-can-and-cant-do/); [Shiftbase](https://www.shiftbase.com/blog/trial-shifts)
- MPs and campaigners have pushed to ban unpaid trial shifts, and TGI Fridays was "named and shamed". — [Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/mps-seek-ban-on-unpaid-trial-shifts); [The Caterer](https://thecaterer.com/all-content/unpaid-trial-shifts-ban-mp-backing); [CIPP](https://www.cipp.org.uk/news-publications/news/tginsuwt.html)
- Australia: the Fair Work Ombudsman has guidance on unpaid work in hospitality. Ireland: unpaid trials are reported to be common in restaurants. — [Fair Work Ombudsman (PDF)](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/sites/default/files/migration/723/Unpaid-work-hospitality-industry.pdf); [Dublin Inquirer](https://www.dublininquirer.com/unpaid-work-trials-are-common-in-the-restaurant-industry.md)

**Teaching and childcare: safer recruitment and safeguarding**
- Under KCSIE, safeguarding must be central to the interview and at least one panel member must have safer-recruitment training. Typical questions are "What would you do if a child disclosed something to you?" and "How would you respond if you were concerned about a colleague's behaviour?". Candidates are also questioned about gaps in employment and vague reasons for leaving. KCSIE 2025 requires online searches of shortlisted candidates. — [SSS Learning](https://ssslearning.co.uk/safeguarding-articles/safer-recruitment-best-practice); [Haringey school policy, Spring 2026 (PDF)](https://campsbourne.haringey.sch.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Statutory-Policy-Safer-Recruitment-for-School-Policy-Spring-2026.pdf); [OpenLearn Create sample questions](https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/mod/resource/view.php?id=220336); [Zen Educate](https://www.zeneducate.com/resources/teaching-hub/safeguarding-interview-questions-for-teachers)

**IT: technical interviews**
- Live coding interviews expect candidates to "think aloud". For non-native speakers this adds a second cognitive task, translating while problem-solving. About ten seconds of silence can read as "stuck". Silence together with an accent can make interviewers assume the candidate did not understand. Advice is to narrate uncertainty ("Let me think through this...") and practise narrating code in English, which is a separate skill from coding or conversation. These are practitioner blogs, some of them commercial. — [DEV: thinking out loud](https://dev.to/rvprasad/what-s-with-thinking-out-loud-in-technical-interviews-2fib); [DEV: non-native speakers](https://dev.to/karuha/how-non-native-english-speakers-can-ace-technical-interviews-5fpi); [f1jobs live coding](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/live-coding-think-aloud-non-native-english); [DesignGurus](https://www.designgurus.io/answers/detail/what-are-the-tips-for-non-native-english-speakers-in-tech-interviews)

**Trades and construction**
- Most UK contractors require a CSCS card to work on site. The Green Labourer card is the most common entry-level card, and employers can check cards online. — [CSCS FAQs](https://www.cscs.uk.com/faqs/); [Simply Business](https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/knowledge/trades/what-is-a-cscs-card/)
- Construction interview questions focus on health and safety and teamwork, for example "How do you ensure you follow health and safety protocols on a busy site?" and "How do you handle disagreements between teams on site?". Irish advice is to mention CSCS, SafePass or trade tickets. — [Randstad construction questions](https://www.randstad.co.uk/career-advice/interview-tips/construction-job-interview-questions); [Randstad skilled trades](https://www.randstad.co.uk/skilled-trades-interview-questions/); [O'Neill & Brennan (IE)](https://www.oneillandbrennan.com/ie/news/common-construction-job-interview-questions/bp450/); [HighSpeed Training](https://www.highspeedtraining.co.uk/hub/interview-questions-for-construction-workers/)

**Visa, sponsorship and right to work**
- Forms and interviews often ask two separate questions: "Do you have the right to work now?" and "Will you need sponsorship now or in the future?". A Graduate visa holder answers Yes / Yes (the visa cannot be extended, so Skilled Worker sponsorship will be needed later). Holders of ILR or settled status answer Yes / No. Not every role can be sponsored, and offers are often conditional on right-to-work checks. — [Hunt UK Visa Sponsors](https://huntukvisasponsors.com/guides/how-to-answer-do-you-need-sponsorship); [When to tell an employer](https://huntukvisasponsors.com/guides/when-to-tell-an-employer-you-need-visa-sponsorship); [UKRI candidate guidance](https://www.ukri.org/about-us/work-for-us/our-employment-policies/right-to-work-in-the-uk-guidance-for-candidates); [Manchester Careers](https://www.careers.manchester.ac.uk/applicationsinterviews/faqs/right-to-work/); [Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/peopleteam/workwithus/righttowork/)
- US career centres also note that international students may be asked about their visa status. — [JMU](https://www.jmu.edu/career/students/communities/international.shtml)

### Inferences
- These formats suggest several lesson strands:
  - (a) values and behavioural narratives for health and care
  - (b) "What would you do if...?" hypotheticals using second conditional / "I would..." and procedural language ("First I'd make sure the person is safe, then I'd report it to...") for care, safeguarding and retail
  - (c) collaborative group-discussion language for assessment days: proposing, agreeing, building on ideas, bringing others in, summarising
  - (d) think-aloud language for IT
  - (e) concise, factual right-to-work answers
- With the overseas care-worker route closed, care-interview lessons in the UK now mainly serve migrants already in the country, for example people switching visas, dependants and refugees. This is an inference from the July 2025 change.

## Q4. Which phrases and functional language do teachers recommend?

### Takeaway
ESL materials consistently prioritise asking for repetition and clarification, rephrasing ("In other words..."), and polite formal forms for work and interviews. Interview-specific advice adds the following:
- slowing your pace
- redirecting instead of apologising when you lose a word
- narrating your thinking to fill silence
- "I" statements with past-tense achievement verbs (STAR)
- framing foreign experience positively

### Cited Findings
- Clarification lesson plans for A2 and B2/C1 sort phrases into "asking for clarification" ("Could you be more specific?", "Just to be clear...") and "clarifying" ("What I'm trying to say is..."). — [Twee lesson plan](https://twee.com/esl-lesson-plans/asking-for-clarification); [ESL Brains](https://eslbrains.com/?p=480408); [Ellii](https://ellii.com//lessons/functional-english/1735)
- Priority phrases: "Could you repeat that?" and "What does ___ mean?". — [learnenglishfeelgood (PDF)](https://www.learnenglishfeelgood.com/lessonplans/b-classroom-language.pdf); [BusyTeacher](https://busyteacher.org/14911-how-to-teach-students-ask-for-clarification.html)
- Phrases for clarifying your own idea: "Let me explain...", "Let me put it another way...", "In other words...", "To put it differently...". — [Mt. SAC worksheet (PDF)](https://jobs.mtsac.edu/llc/passportrewards/llctutoring/sdla/level3to6_veslamla/fillable_EC03_AskingforClarification.pdf)
- Polite forms suit employers and new contacts, so use formal rather than casual versions in interviews. — [BusyTeacher](https://busyteacher.org/18655-comprehension-check-5-essential-tips.html); [All Ears English](https://www.allearsenglish.com/?p=49996)
- Confirming questions (checking you understood the question before answering) are taught as a speaking-test strategy. — [The English Farm](https://theenglishfarm.com/es/courses/speaking-test-strategies/speaking-test-strategies-16-confirming-questions)
- For remote interviews: slow down about 10–15%, and replace apologies with redirection when you lose a word. — [TalkDrill](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/interview/remote-job-interview-english-tips/)
- For technical interviews: use thinking fillers ("Let me think through this...") and narrate confusion rather than going silent. — [f1jobs live coding](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/live-coding-think-aloud-non-native-english); [DEV](https://dev.to/karuha/how-non-native-english-speakers-can-ace-technical-interviews-5fpi)
- For behavioural answers: use first-person action verbs and don't hide behind "we". — [uni2study](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/us-job-interview-norms-and-behavioral-questions-for-international-students); [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/international-students-interview-prep-career-centers/)

### Inferences
Suggested functional language syllabus, drawn from the sources above:
1. **Clarifying and checking:** "Sorry, could you repeat the question?", "Do you mean...?", "Just to check I understand — you'd like an example of...?"
2. **Buying time:** "That's a good question — let me think for a moment.", "Let me think through this."
3. **STAR narrative:**
   - past simple for actions ("I organised...")
   - past continuous for background ("I was working as...")
   - past perfect for earlier events ("I had already...")
   - result language ("As a result...", "This meant that...")
   - reflection ("Looking back, I learned...", "Next time I would...")
4. **Present perfect for experience:** "I've worked in care for five years."
5. **Hypotheticals:** "If a resident refused medication, I would...", "The first thing I'd do is..."
6. **Hedging and diplomacy:** "I'd say one area I'm working on is...", "I tend to..." (for weakness questions), "I'm open to discussing salary — my research suggests a range of..."
7. **Reframing foreign experience:** "In my country, I worked as..., which is equivalent to...", "The skills I used there transfer directly to..."
8. **Group-task language:** "Shall we start by...?", "Building on what X said...", "What do you think, Y?", "So, to summarise..."
9. **Questions to ask the interviewer**, and polite closings.
10. **Right to work:** "I have the right to work in the UK until [date] on a Graduate visa, and I would need sponsorship after that."

### Gaps
- I found no published ESL job-interview syllabus that grounds its phrase lists in research (for example from British Council, NATECLA or TESOL). The phrase lists above are mostly from generic clarification lessons, adapted by inference.
- I found no research on which functional phrases most improve interviewer ratings.
