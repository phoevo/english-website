# Build care, hospitality and retail interviews next

Synomilo's next job-interview conversations should follow the jobs adult migrants actually apply for, not the white-collar roles that dominate existing ESL material. **Health and social care is the most consistent migrant-heavy sector in all five countries researched, with hospitality and food service close behind.** Retail, cleaning, warehouse work and logistics come next. Publicly funded ESOL-for-work courses in the UK, US, Canada and Australia focus on the same sectors. Free ESL lessons, by contrast, are almost all generic ("tell me about yourself", strengths and weaknesses), and dialogue lessons built around one specific role are rare. The best opening is a set of role-specific conversations: care worker, hotel receptionist, restaurant server on a trial shift, supermarket assessment day, warehouse phone screen. Each should be built around the interview format that role really uses. Synomilo already has a B2 generic guide, which is a front-end developer interview covering tell-me-about-yourself, weaknesses, deadlines, five-year plans and "any questions for us?". It also has a B2 newly qualified nurse ward interview. So the nursing and IT ideas below are framed as complements: an experienced overseas nurse on a video panel, and a live-coding think-aloud round. The evidence is directional, not measured. Several key sites (Preply, ISL Collective, ESL Brains, the Migration Observatory, BLS, canada.ca) could not be fetched, Reddit returned nothing, and some figures are second-hand.

## Migrants cluster in care, hospitality and support jobs everywhere

The labour-market data is consistent across countries. In the UK, migrants are over-represented in **administrative and support services, hospitality, and health and care**, and recent growth in migrant employment came mainly from care work ([Migration Observatory 2026](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/2026-Briefing-Migrants-in-the-UK-labour-market-an-overview.pdf)). One secondary source puts non-British nationals at **42% of care workers** in England's adult social care ([Access Group](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/blog/hsc-international-care-workers-in-the-uk/)). Treat that figure as unverified: an earlier Care England briefing gave a much lower "from overseas" share ([Care England](https://www.careengland.org.uk/social-cares-improving-workforce-figures-mask-a-growing-concern-with-no-plan-to-address/)). In Ireland, non-Irish nationals hold **45.6% of administrative and support jobs and 45.1% of accommodation and food jobs** ([CSO 2024](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-den/distributionofearningsbynationality2024)). In Australia, accommodation and food services has the highest share of recent migrants, at 14.3% ([Ai Group](https://www.australianindustrygroup.com.au/resourcecentre/research-economics/economics-intelligence/2026/the-contribution-of-recent-migrants-to-australias-workforce/)). In Canada, immigrants are **42% of nurse aides** ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/immigration-economy.html)), and recent arrivals work disproportionately as nurse aides, truck drivers, food and beverage servers and retail salespeople ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2024009/article/00005-eng.htm)). US foreign-born workers are over-represented in service occupations, in construction, and in production and transport ([BLS 2025](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/forbrn_05192026.htm)). At the professional end, immigrant-origin workers make up 38% of the US STEM workforce ([MPI](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/research/future-work-immigrant-origin-workers-us-economy)).

ESOL provision follows the same pattern. Over half of US Integrated Education and Training programmes prepare learners for the **Certified Nursing Assistant** credential, though that survey dates from about 2017 ([CLASP](https://www.clasp.org/publications/report/brief/integrated-education-and-training-career-pathways-policy-practice/)). UK colleges pair ESOL with health and social care, retail or hospitality ([Halesowen](https://www.halesowen.ac.uk/study/course/esol-skills-for-employment-entry-level-3)), and WEA runs an ESOL course specifically for care homes ([WEA](https://wea.org.uk/courses/esol/8-october-esol-employability-care-homes)). Policy has changed who these learners are. The UK closed overseas recruitment of care workers on 22 July 2025 ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uk-care-worker-visa-route-closes)), and about **95% of international recruits into care are now people already in the UK** on dependant, family or student visas ([Care Workers' Charity](https://www.thecareworkerscharity.org.uk/?p=20025)). The typical learner is therefore applying to a local employer from inside the country, not being interviewed overseas by a recruiter. Most courses run at Entry 3 to Level 1 (roughly A2–B1), which is below Synomilo's B2 default. That is a reason to grade several of the new conversations at B1.

The supply side shows the gap. Teaching-material sites carry many generic interview lessons at A2–C2. Where a specific role appears, it is usually an example inside a generic lesson, such as the nurse profile in Onestopenglish's lesson ([Onestopenglish](https://onestopenglish.com/language-for-/language-for-job-interviews/558165.article)). The only hotel interview material found was a short listening task in a Canadian LINC workbook ([NorQuest](https://openeducationalberta.ca/norquest-lincworks-interviews-clb3/?p=64)). Care-worker interview advice exists mostly as native-speaker employer content ([Randstad](https://www.randstad.co.uk/career-advice/career-guidance/top-care-worker-interview-questions-answers-how-to-prepare-for-a-job/)) and one niche app blog ([SpeakShark](https://speakshark.com/blog/caregiver-job-interview-english-practice-uk)). On tutor marketplaces, demand is real but not broken down by role. Preply's interview-prep tutor listing runs past page 1,300 ([Preply](https://preply.com/en/online/tutors-english-job-interview-prep?page=1308)), and tutors usually pitch interview prep alongside business and finance English ([Preply](https://preply.com/en/online/tutors-english-job-interview-prep?page=18)). That is a supply signal more than a demand measurement.

## Sixteen conversations ranked by demand and gap

The ranking weighs three things: how many migrant learners work in the role, how thin existing free dialogue material is, and how distinct the conversation is from Synomilo's two guides. The first six combine large migrant workforces with almost no graded dialogues. Ideas 7 to 12 serve smaller or more specialised groups. Ideas 13 to 16 are narrower or partly overlap with existing guides.

### Tier one: large workforces, almost no dialogues

**1. Care worker in a UK care home, applying from inside the UK (B1).** This role has the strongest combined signal. Care is the main source of migrant job growth in the UK ([Migration Observatory](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/2026-Briefing-Migrants-in-the-UK-labour-market-an-overview.pdf)), ESOL courses are built around care homes ([WEA](https://wea.org.uk/courses/esol/8-october-esol-employability-care-homes)), and almost all recruits now apply from inside the UK ([Care Workers' Charity](https://www.thecareworkerscharity.org.uk/?p=20025)). The interview is values-based. The standard topics are "Why care?", safeguarding, dignity, active listening, de-escalation, record-keeping and manual handling ([Birdie](https://birdie.care/blog/carer-interview-questions-what-should-you-ask); [CareLearning](https://carelearning.org.uk/care-careers/care-worker-interview-questions-and-answers-with-examples/)). Behavioural "tell me about a time" questions are preferred to hypothetical ones ([Flourish](https://flourish.co.uk/community/care-support-worker-interview-questions/)). Include "Why do you want to work in care?", "Tell me about a time you helped someone who was upset", "What would you do if a resident refused their medication?", "What would you do if you suspected abuse?", questions about shift and weekend availability, and a short, factual right-to-work answer. This complements the nursing guide by moving from clinical judgement to personal care and values. *Hook: Amira cared for her grandmother for years in Syria, and now the manager wants to know whether that counts as experience.*

**2. Hotel front-desk receptionist (B1).** Hospitality is the most migrant-heavy sector in Ireland and Australia ([CSO](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-den/distributionofearningsbynationality2024); [Ai Group](https://www.australianindustrygroup.com.au/resourcecentre/research-economics/economics-intelligence/2026/the-contribution-of-recent-migrants-to-australias-workforce/)). It appears in ESOL courses in all four ESOL systems researched ([City Lit](https://www.citylit.ac.uk/courses/workplace-communication-skills-how-to-get-a-better-job-esol-entry-3-level-1); [London ESOL Plus](https://www.london.gov.uk/programmes-strategies/communities-and-social-justice/migrants-and-refugees/english-esol-classes-london/esol-plus-employment-hub)). Yet the only hotel interview material found was a short CLB 3 listening task ([NorQuest](https://openeducationalberta.ca/norquest-lincworks-interviews-clb3/?p=64)). Include customer-service experience, "A guest says their room is dirty and there are no others available. What do you do?", working night shifts, languages spoken as an asset, and a short in-interview role-play where the manager plays a guest. *Hook: halfway through the interview the manager stops asking questions and becomes an angry guest at the desk.*

**3. Internationally educated nurse at an NHS video panel interview (B2–C1).** Nursing is where learner anxiety and a high-stakes recruitment pipeline meet. Overseas new-graduate nurses describe being "really nervous" about English-language interviews ([Incredible Health](https://www.incrediblehealth.com/nurse-advice/questions/ba5f77c0/hi-i-am-a-new-grad-an-international-nurse-and); [allnurses](https://allnurses.com/first-rn-job-anxious-about-t706190)). Trusts use competency questions and NHS-values questions answered in STAR form, sometimes over video ([NHS Professionals](https://www.nhsprofessionals.nhs.uk/-/media/corporate/nhsp-international/nursing-and-midwifery-application-support-pack.pdf); [ID Medical](https://www.id-medical.com/international-nurses-guide-to-preparing-for-your-nhs-nursing-interview-5-questions-with-example-answers/)). Health and social care was also the largest employment-permit sector in Ireland in 2025, at 26% ([Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/arid-41932158.html)). To avoid repeating the existing guide, make the candidate an experienced nurse from abroad facing a three-person panel by video. Use published values questions such as "Tell me about a time you made a difference to someone's day" ([Mersey Care](https://merseycare.nhs.uk/values-based-questions)), plus reframing foreign experience ("In the Philippines I worked on a 40-bed surgical ward, which is similar to..."), and repair phrases for a bad connection. UK nursing visa grants have fallen sharply ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-work)), so an Irish or Australian setting would be more realistic. *Hook: Grace has eight years of ICU experience in Manila, three panel members on screen, and a connection that keeps freezing.*

**4. Restaurant server or barista: the interview and the trial-shift offer (A2–B1).** Food and beverage servers are a typical first job for recent arrivals in Canada ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2024009/article/00005-eng.htm)), and 30.2% of Ukrainians working in Ireland in 2023 were in accommodation and food ([CSO](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-den/distributionofearningsbynationality2024)). The trial shift is a distinctive real-world step. UK law on unpaid trials is a grey area ([Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2024/dont-fall-foul-of-minimum-wage-with-unpaid-trial-shifts-in-the-hospitality-industry/)), MPs have pushed for a ban ([Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/mps-seek-ban-on-unpaid-trial-shifts)), and unpaid trials are reported to be common in Dublin restaurants ([Dublin Inquirer](https://www.dublininquirer.com/unpaid-work-trials-are-common-in-the-restaurant-industry.md)). Include short availability questions, "How would you handle a customer who says their food is cold?", working under pressure, and the polite pragmatics of asking "Will the trial shift be paid?" without sounding difficult. *Hook: the interview goes well, the owner says "Come in Saturday for a trial", and Tomás has to decide whether to ask if he'll be paid.*

**5. Supermarket assistant at an assessment day (B1–B2).** Retail salespeople are a common recent-immigrant job in Canada ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2024009/article/00005-eng.htm)), and retail is a core ESOL vocational strand in the UK ([Haringey](https://new.haringey.gov.uk/jobs-training/haringey-learns/courses/search/esol-skills-life-work-entry-3-employability-skills)). The format is the interesting part. Candidates describe group tasks that involve prioritising a day's problems (missing stock, breakages, unhappy customers) ([Glassdoor Waitrose](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Waitrose-Interview-E22047-RVW17051001.htm)), and Tesco assessors look for clear speaking, listening and stepping up to lead ([Practice Aptitude Tests](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/resources/what-to-expect-at-a-tesco-assessment-centre/)). This makes a natural multi-speaker conversation: four candidates plan together, then one goes into a short one-to-one interview. It practises proposing, building on ideas, bringing a quiet person in, and summarising. *Hook: four strangers have ten minutes to sort out a disastrous Saturday in the shop, and the assessor is watching who listens.*

**6. Warehouse picker or packer: agency phone screen (A2–B1).** Warehouse and fulfilment jobs are where US resettlement agencies place newcomers first ([LSS Minnesota](https://www.lssmn.org/services/refugees/services/employment)), and warehousing is among the largest occupations for EU-born workers in the UK ([Migration Observatory](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/resources/briefings/migrants-in-the-uk-labour-market-an-overview)). Phone interviews feature in Canadian LINC curricula ([Settlement.org LINC 4](https://settlement.org/downloads/linc/LCG1to5/Tm9LINC4.pdf)). Include availability for night and weekend shifts, how you will get to the site, right to work, lifting and safety, and above all phone repair language ("Sorry, could you say the address again?"). Flag one limit to the writer: no reliable source on the real format of warehouse agency interviews was found, so keep the details generic. *Hook: the agency calls Dawit while he's on the bus, and he has two minutes to show he's reliable.*

### Tier two: distinct groups with thin coverage

**7. Software developer: the live-coding round (B2–C1).** ICT made up 12% of Irish employment permits in 2025 ([Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/arid-41932158.html)), and recent migrants hold 21.3% of Australian professional roles, IT among them ([Ai Group](https://www.australianindustrygroup.com.au/resourcecentre/research-economics/economics-intelligence/2026/the-contribution-of-recent-migrants-to-australias-workforce/)). ESL material for this role exists only in pieces ([Twee](https://twee.com/esl-lesson-plans/job?page=2); [EnglishPost](https://englishpost.org/lesson-plans/lesson-tips-for-a-job-interview)). The existing generic guide already features a front-end developer in a behavioural interview, so this one should be the **technical round**. Practitioner blogs say about ten seconds of silence reads as "stuck", and that narrating code in English is a separate skill ([f1jobs](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/live-coding-think-aloud-non-native-english); [DEV](https://dev.to/karuha/how-non-native-english-speakers-can-ace-technical-interviews-5fpi)). Include clarifying the problem, thinking aloud ("Let me think through this..."), explaining trade-offs, admitting uncertainty, and responding to hints. This could be framed as Daniel's second-round interview, as a direct sequel to the generic guide. *Hook: Daniel passed round one, and now he has to solve a problem on a shared screen while explaining every step out loud.*

**8. Hotel housekeeper or office cleaner, through an agency (A2–B1).** Administrative and support services, which includes cleaning and agency work, is migrant-heavy in both the UK ([Migration Observatory](https://migrationobservatory.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/2026-Briefing-Migrants-in-the-UK-labour-market-an-overview.pdf)) and Ireland, where non-Irish nationals hold 45.6% of these jobs ([CSO](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-den/distributionofearningsbynationality2024)). No ESL interview material for this role was found. Include early-morning availability, working alone, what to do if you find a guest's valuables, chemicals and safety, and a reference from a previous employer. *Hook: the supervisor asks one simple question, "What would you do if you found a wallet under the bed?", and the answer matters more than any CV.*

**9. Teaching assistant or nursery practitioner (B1–B2).** One Preply tutor advertises mock interviews for education, community and social services roles ([Preply tutor](https://preply.com/en/tutor/3316780)). UK schools must put safeguarding at the centre of the interview. Typical questions are "What would you do if a child disclosed something to you?" and questions about gaps in employment ([SSS Learning](https://ssslearning.co.uk/safeguarding-articles/safer-recruitment-best-practice); [Zen Educate](https://www.zeneducate.com/resources/teaching-hub/safeguarding-interview-questions-for-teachers)). Include a disclosure scenario, a question about concerns over a colleague, explaining a career gap after migration, and the second-conditional procedural language these questions call for. *Hook: Lena expected questions about lesson ideas, but the panel keeps asking what she would do if something went wrong.*

**10. Nurse aide or CNA in a US or Canadian care home (B1).** This is the North American version of idea 1, justified by its own evidence: over half of US IET programmes target the CNA credential ([CLASP](https://www.clasp.org/publications/report/brief/integrated-education-and-training-career-pathways-policy-practice/)), colleges run CNA programmes for English learners ([Hawkeye](https://www.hawkeyecollege.edu/community/adult-education/integrated-education-and-training)), and immigrants are 42% of Canadian nurse aides ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/immigration-economy.html)). Use North American vocabulary (resident, shift, charge nurse, certification) and cover the same values and scenario questions as idea 1. If Synomilo wants only one care conversation, merge this into idea 1 and add a vocabulary note on regional differences. *Hook: Joseph has just passed his CNA exam, and the director of nursing wants to know how he'd calm a confused resident at 3 a.m.*

**11. Construction labourer or electrician (B1).** Trades are an immigration priority in Canada's Express Entry ([CIC News](https://www.cicnews.com/2025/12/these-are-the-types-of-candidates-ircc-is-focusing-on-in-2026-1263531.html)) and in Ireland's May 2026 changes to its permit lists ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en-IE/insights/2026/05/27/irelands-employment-permit-occupations-lists-whats-changed-in-may-2026)). US foreign-born workers are over-represented in construction ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/forbrn_05202025.htm)). The UK is the exception: about 85% of construction workers there are UK-born ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2026/02/18/families-of-migrant-workers-drive-real-change-in-uk-workforce-report-finds)), so set this one in Ireland or Canada. Interviews focus on health and safety, teamwork on site and safety cards such as CSCS or SafePass ([Randstad](https://www.randstad.co.uk/career-advice/interview-tips/construction-job-interview-questions); [O'Neill & Brennan](https://www.oneillandbrennan.com/ie/news/common-construction-job-interview-questions/bp450/)). *Hook: Marek's qualifications are Polish, the site manager's questions are all about safety, and the word "ticket" doesn't mean what he thinks.*

**12. Engineer or accountant with foreign qualifications and "no local experience" (B2–C1).** This idea targets the gatekeeping problem described in the research. Celia Roberts found a "linguistic penalty" in UK selection interviews, especially in how foreign work experience is recognised or dismissed ([Diversities 2012](https://newdiversities.mmg.mpg.de/fileadmin/user_upload/2012_14-02_art4.pdf)). WES advises employers that "tell me about yourself" can fail immigrant candidates ([WES](https://www.wes.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/0622-4-interviewing-immigrant-candidates-v4.pdf)). Canadian guidance tells candidates to describe their international experience rather than apologise for lacking local experience ([immigration.ca](https://immigration.ca/interview-techniques-can-eliminate-hidden-cultural-biases/)). Include "We see you don't have Canadian experience...", explaining qualification equivalence, mapping past projects onto the employer's competency vocabulary, and "I" rather than "we" in achievement stories ([Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/international-students-interview-prep-career-centers/)). This builds on the generic guide's "your previous role" questions but adds the reframing skill. *Hook: Priya managed a bridge project in Chennai, and the interviewer's first question is whether she has worked "here" before.*

### Tier three: narrower audiences or partial overlap

**13. Graduate on a Graduate visa: one-way video round and sponsorship call (B2).** This conversation has two parts. In the first, the candidate prepares for and records answers in a one-way video interview, an increasingly common screening format that removes any chance to ask for clarification ([ESP Today 2026](https://www.esptodayjournal.org/pdf/january_2026/1_Fei-Wen_Cheng.pdf)). In the second, a recruiter calls and asks the two separate right-to-work questions, "Can you work now?" and "Will you need sponsorship?" ([Hunt UK Visa Sponsors](https://huntukvisasponsors.com/guides/how-to-answer-do-you-need-sponsorship)). It extends the generic guide into formats that guide does not cover. *Hook: the screen says "You have 30 seconds to prepare", and nobody is there to ask what the question means.*

**14. Truck or delivery driver (B1).** Immigrants are 35% of Canadian truck-transportation workers ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/immigration-economy.html)), transport is a 2026 Express Entry category ([Redim](https://redim.ca/5-new-express-entry-category-based-draws-will-run-in-2026-targeting-doctors-researchers-managers-transport-and-military/)), and Ireland's 2026 list changes covered transport ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en-IE/insights/2026/05/27/irelands-employment-permit-occupations-lists-whats-changed-in-may-2026)). Include driving record, long hours and fatigue, delays and customer complaints at delivery, and route problems. No source on the driver interview format itself was found, so this is lower confidence. *Hook: the dispatcher asks Arjun what he'd do if his GPS sent him down a road too low for the truck.*

**15. International medical graduate interviewing for an NHS doctor post (C1).** General medical practitioners were the top occupation for UK Skilled Worker grants to overseas applicants in the year to June 2026, with 3,126 grants, according to a third-party tracker of Home Office data ([Hunt UK Visa Sponsors](https://huntukvisasponsors.com/skilled-worker-visa-statistics)). Canada ran its first draw for physicians in February 2026 ([Moving2Canada](https://moving2canada.com/news/2026-express-entry-categories/)). International medical graduates show differential attainment, linked partly to unfamiliar UK norms ([Woolf et al., BMJ Open](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1527438/1/e013429.full.pdf)). The audience is small and the required level is high, and interview format details were not confirmed from GMC sources. Include a clinical-governance scenario, a disagreement with a senior colleague, and explaining your training pathway. *Hook: Dr Okafor knows the medicine cold, but the panel's question is about a time she challenged a consultant.*

**16. Factory or food-processing operative (A2).** US foreign-born workers are over-represented in production work ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/forbrn_05192026.htm)), resettlement agencies place newcomers in manufacturing ([LSS Minnesota](https://www.lssmn.org/services/refugees/services/employment)), and employers report higher retention among refugee workers ([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2020/05/hiring-refugees-report-economic-sense/)). This is the lowest-level idea: short questions, shift patterns, following safety rules, and saying "I don't understand" safely. It would also be Synomilo's first A2 interview conversation. *Hook: the supervisor speaks fast over the machine noise, and Fatima has to check she's understood the start time.*

## Interviews test genre and format, not just grammar

The strongest research finding is that migrant candidates are penalised less for their English than for not knowing how the answer is supposed to sound. Campbell and Roberts found that recruiters judged overseas candidates as failing to blend personal talk ("who I am") with the organisation's competency vocabulary ([Roberts, AEDEAN plenary](https://aedean.org/pdf_atatimecrisis/PLENARIA_Roberts_AEDEAN35.pdf)). Earlier *Crosstalk* work found that short answers led interviewers to take control of the conversation ([LGPolicy summary](https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/lgpolicy-list/2005-September/002329.html)). Every conversation above should therefore show a full STAR answer: name the value, tell a "time when" story in the past tenses, give the result, reflect on it. Each one should also include at least one answer the tutor can stretch from one sentence to a minute or two. Self-promotion needs explicit permission. Career staff report that international students feel it is "bragging" ([Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/news/blog/2021/04/16/international-student-recruiting/)), and crediting the team can read as low confidence ([uni2study](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/us-job-interview-norms-and-behavioral-questions-for-international-students)).

Several other formats and skills can be built into the conversations rather than given lessons of their own. **Questions to ask the interviewer** is the single biggest interview search intent in the UK, at about 17,520 searches a month in a study of 63,000 monthly interview-question searches, ahead of common questions and strengths and weaknesses ([Ciphr](https://www.ciphr.com/press-releases/new-google-study-reveals-the-most-searched-for-interview-questions)). Every conversation should end with the candidate asking a role-appropriate question. **Phone and video repair language** matters because audio-only and video formats strip out visual cues and add lag ([Oxford Careers](https://www.careers.ox.ac.uk/node/768726)). Coaches advise slowing down by 10–15% and redirecting rather than apologising when a word is lost ([TalkDrill](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/interview/remote-job-interview-english-tips/)). **One-way recorded interviews** call for paraphrasing the question back into the answer and planning a STAR outline in the preparation time. The evidence that AI scoring is biased against accents is weak: it rests on vendor claims, a commissioned audit and anecdotes ([HireVue](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/hiring/the-latest-leap-in-hirevues-assessment-technology); [ORCAA audit](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/oneil-risk-consulting-and-algorithmic-auditing-01-2021.pdf)). **Hypothetical "what would you do if" questions** need second-conditional and step-by-step language for care, safeguarding and retail. **Group-task language** fits assessment days. **Factual right-to-work answers** belong in nearly every migrant scenario ([UKRI](https://www.ukri.org/about-us/work-for-us/our-employment-policies/right-to-work-in-the-uk-guidance-for-candidates)). **Reframing foreign experience** ("which is equivalent to...") counters the penalty Roberts describes. Salary talk has search interest, though far lower at about 440 a month in the Ciphr data, and no research on non-native speakers. It can be a short closing exchange in the professional-level ideas.

## Evidence limits

The ranking is a reasoned synthesis, not a measured demand ranking. No learner survey ranking requested professions was found, and the research did not obtain search volumes for role-specific ESL phrases such as "care worker interview English". Popularity counts from ISL Collective, ESL Brains, BusyTeacher and Linguahouse could not be retrieved, so the claim that role-specific dialogues are scarce rests on search results and needs confirming by browsing those catalogues. Preply's listing size measures how many tutors offer interview prep, not how many learners want it. Reddit returned no results at all. Several primary statistical sites (Migration Observatory, BLS, canada.ca, Jobs and Skills Australia) could not be fetched, so their figures come from search summaries. Some key numbers are secondary or conflicting: the 42% UK care-worker share, the 42% versus 35% Canadian nurse-aide share, the UK doctor visa grants, and the Express Entry details. The US CNA figure is from about 2017. Celia Roberts' papers were read only in summary. No reliable source described warehouse, agency or driver interview formats, and the IT think-aloud advice comes from practitioner and partly commercial blogs. Policy is moving quickly (UK care visa closure, Canadian low-wage permit freezes, record-low US refugee admissions), so any country-specific detail in a conversation should be checked before publishing.

## Conclusion

The gap is less about which questions get asked than about who is answering them and in what format. Generic interview English is saturated. What is missing is the care-home manager, the hotel duty manager, the agency recruiter on the phone, and the assessor at a group task, each speaking at the B1 level most migrant job-seekers actually have. Pitching some conversations at B1, and one at A2, would also widen Synomilo's audience beyond its B2 default, towards the ESOL-for-work learners who make up the largest group.

There is a second opportunity in sequencing. The existing generic developer guide and nursing guide can become the first episodes of short series: Daniel's live-coding round, then the experienced overseas nurse on a video panel. Tutors would get continuity, and the site could reuse characters. Across all the conversations, the most valuable thing to teach is how answers are expected to sound: the STAR structure, the employer's competency vocabulary, and reframing experience from abroad. Grammar matters less, because the research shows migrant candidates are mostly judged on these genre rules.
